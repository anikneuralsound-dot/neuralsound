# Karaoke Take or Spoken Demo? Which NeuralSound Page to Open

NeuralSound has a few tools with similar names, and the right one depends on what you're making. If you want to sing over a song, you need one page. If you recorded yourself talking with music in the background, you need another. This is a short guide to picking the right one the first time.

## For a karaoke take

A karaoke take needs the song without the lead singer, so your voice can sit where theirs used to be. Open the [vocal remover](https://neuralsound.org/vocal-remover). You upload the track and get the full backing back, with drums, bass, guitars and keys still in place but no lead vocal.

Before you record, it helps to learn the part properly. The [instrumental remover](https://neuralsound.org/instrumental-remover) does the opposite job: it takes the band away and leaves the voice on its own, so you can hear the exact melody, the phrasing and where the singer breathes. Listen to that a few times, then switch to the backing track and sing it yourself.

If you want a very clean a cappella to study, the [vocal isolator](https://neuralsound.org/vocal-isolator) works harder on the reverb and cymbals that tend to leak into the voice.

## For a spoken demo

A spoken demo is a different job. Think of a product walkthrough, a recorded lesson or a voice-over where a track was playing while you talked. Here you want to keep the speech and lose the music, so open the [background music remover](https://neuralsound.org/background-music-remover). It's tuned for the speaking voice rather than singing, which keeps your words clear while the music comes out from underneath.

It's also handy when you want to change the music later. Take the original track out first, and you have a clean voice to put new music under.

## A quick way to decide

Ask yourself what you want to keep.

- Keep the band: use the vocal remover.
- Keep the singer: use the instrumental remover.
- Keep your own speaking voice: use the background music remover.

If you need every part separately, [music separation](https://neuralsound.org/tools/music-separation) splits a song into vocals, drums, bass and the rest.

## A few small tips

Start with the best copy of the file you have. A clean, high-quality source separates noticeably better than a phone recording of speakers.

Listen back on headphones before you share anything. The quiet gaps between phrases are where leftover sound shows up first.

And keep it fair. Practising at home is one thing, but if you post a karaoke cover or a demo with someone else's music in it, make sure you have the rights to use it.

NeuralSound works in the browser and on [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.neuralsound.musicseparation) and [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neuralsound-ai-vocal-remover/id6756906827). Questions are welcome at support@neuralsound.org.
